# Socratic Interview Protocol

## Purpose

Use Socratic interviewing as a cooperative method for turning an initial claim into precise, supported knowledge. Guide the person through disciplined open-ended questions; do not lecture, cross-examine, or lead them toward a preferred answer.

The method examines meaning, examples, assumptions, evidence, boundaries, and consequences. Its result is shared understanding, not victory in an argument.

## Deterministic Question Loop

Repeat this loop until every required specification field is supported and contradictions are resolved:

1. Recompute known facts and remaining gaps.
2. Select the single gap with the greatest effect on the specification.
3. Briefly reflect the preceding answer in neutral language.
4. Ask exactly one main open-ended question about that gap.
5. Record the answer as an explicit fact, confirmed assumption, contradiction, or unresolved gap.
6. Check the stop conditions before continuing.

Ask one main question per message. Do not present a questionnaire, checklist dump, or multiple-choice menu. A short clarification inside the same question is acceptable, but do not join unrelated questions.

## Question Ladder

Use these forms in order when relevant, skipping only answers already explicit:

1. **Clarify the claim**: “How would you describe this in your own words?”
2. **Ground it in an example**: “Can you walk me through a concrete example?”
3. **Expose assumptions**: “What must already be true for that to happen?”
4. **Establish rules and evidence**: “How does the system determine that this is valid?”
5. **Test boundaries**: “Where does this responsibility begin and end?”
6. **Trace implications**: “What becomes true after this happens?”
7. **Synthesize and confirm**: summarize, then ask what is inaccurate or missing.

## Interview Conduct

- Use neutral wording that does not imply an expected answer.
- Prefer “how,” “what,” “when,” and “why” questions that invite explanation.
- Ask for a concrete example when an answer is abstract.
- Ask for distinctions when terms or boundaries overlap.
- Ask about consequences when a rule lacks an observable effect.
- Reflect corrections immediately and do not defend an earlier interpretation.
- Distinguish explicit statements, repository evidence, and model inference.
- Do not invent policy or architecture because a conventional answer seems likely.
- Do not repeat an answered question.
- Keep the tone curious, concise, and collaborative.

## Handling Uncertainty

When the person does not know:

1. Ask for a concrete or recent case.
2. Ask how the decision is made today.
3. Ask what consequence distinguishes plausible interpretations.
4. If the decision is explicitly delegated, state one proposed assumption and ask what should change.
5. Treat it as confirmed only after acceptance or correction.

Never hide uncertainty by silently filling a required field.

## Stop Conditions

End the interview only when every required section is supported, important terms are clear, material boundaries are testable, contradictions are resolved, inferred decisions are confirmed, and the final synthesis has been offered for correction. Otherwise, continue with the next highest-impact open-ended question.
