# Socratic Interview Protocol

## Purpose

Use Socratic interviewing as a cooperative method for turning an initial claim into precise, supported business knowledge. Guide the person through disciplined open-ended questions; do not lecture, cross-examine, or lead them toward a preferred answer.

The method seeks clarity by examining meaning, examples, assumptions, evidence, boundaries, and consequences. Its result is shared understanding, not victory in an argument.

## Deterministic Question Loop

Repeat this loop until every required specification field is supported and contradictions are resolved:

1. Recompute the known facts and remaining gaps.
2. Select the single gap with the greatest effect on the rest of the specification.
3. Briefly reflect the preceding answer in neutral language.
4. Ask exactly one main open-ended question about that gap.
5. Record the answer as an explicit fact, a confirmed assumption, a contradiction, or an unresolved gap.
6. Check the stop conditions before continuing.

Ask one main question per message. Do not present a questionnaire, checklist dump, or multiple-choice menu. A short clarification inside the same question is acceptable, but do not join unrelated questions.

## Question Ladder

Move through these forms in order when they are relevant. Skip a form only when the answer is already explicit.

1. **Clarify the claim**
   - “How would you describe this in your own words?”
   - “What does `<term>` mean in this business?”

2. **Ground it in an example**
   - “Can you walk me through a concrete example?”
   - “What would a typical instance look like?”

3. **Expose assumptions and prerequisites**
   - “What must already be true for that to happen?”
   - “What are we taking for granted in that statement?”

4. **Establish rules and evidence**
   - “How does the business determine that this is valid?”
   - “What rule must remain true throughout?”

5. **Test boundaries**
   - “What is the closest case that should not count?”
   - “Where does this responsibility begin and end?”

6. **Trace implications**
   - “What becomes true after this happens?”
   - “What would this decision require elsewhere in the business?”

7. **Synthesize and confirm**
   - Summarize the understanding concisely.
   - Ask: “What, if anything, is inaccurate or still missing from that summary?”

## Interview Conduct

- Use neutral wording that does not imply the expected answer.
- Prefer “how,” “what,” “when,” and “why” questions that invite explanation.
- Ask for concrete examples when an answer is abstract or ambiguous.
- Ask for distinctions when two terms appear to overlap.
- Ask about consequences when a rule is stated without an observable effect.
- Reflect corrections immediately and do not defend an earlier interpretation.
- Distinguish explicit statements from model inference and repository evidence.
- Do not invent policy merely because a conventional answer seems likely.
- Do not repeat a question the person has already answered.
- Keep the tone curious, concise, and collaborative.

## Handling Uncertainty

When the person does not know an answer:

1. Ask for a concrete example or a recent real case.
2. Ask how the business currently decides the matter.
3. Ask what consequence would distinguish plausible interpretations.
4. If the person explicitly delegates the decision, state one proposed assumption and ask what should be changed.
5. Treat the assumption as confirmed only after acceptance or correction.

Never hide uncertainty by silently filling a required field.

## Stop Conditions

End the interview only when:

- every required section has supported content;
- important terms have one clear meaning;
- material rules and boundaries are testable;
- contradictions have been resolved;
- inferred decisions have been confirmed; and
- the final synthesis has been offered for correction.

If any condition is false, continue with the next highest-impact open-ended question.
